## Two books: Pluto and Manual, in dollars instead of a percentage (Marcelo 2026-11-05; built, staging cut-over pending)
Plain language: the account is split into two books, Pluto and Manual (all manual trading, hedges included), each with its own dollar balance that grows or shrinks with its own results. Today Pluto's share is a percentage of the whole account, so manual losses shrink Pluto's budget and can halt it. Each book will only spend its own cash; its limits and Pluto's daily-loss breaker are measured on its own value.
- Cash comes from IBKR's nightly statement (Flex), each row posted to a book: trades and assignments by position, dividends by who held the shares on the ex-date, interest by each book's daily cash balance, shared fees and unmatched rows pro rata to book value (unmatched rows flagged ⚠️ for re-assignment). Intraday: today's fills and detected assignments are provisional until the statement arrives; a gap against IBKR's live cash sends an alert only.
- Deposits and withdrawals wait for an admin to split them, in a Dashboard widget shown only while one is pending.
- A ticker switched between books moves its positions at the mark: the source book realises its P&L, the receiver gets them as just opened and pays the mark value in cash. A receiver left with negative free cash can't open until it's positive again.
- Each order's limits (max order %, concentration, min cash reserve) and Pluto's daily-loss breaker are measured against its own book. Cut-over at a nightly close, with Pluto's starting value set by Marcelo and the rest of the cash going to Manual.
- Screens (Marcelo 2026-11-05: Juan must not read the account's cash as his): every figure someone trades from shows the order's own book; a book switch on the Dashboard (everyone starts on All books; nothing about either book is hidden from traders, Marcelo 2026-11-05; only splitting deposits and moving cash are admin-only; the pending deposit card is admins only, since traders can't act on it, and traders see the unallocated cash in Account Value, Risk & Limits and Pulse); Pulse splits Available Cash by book; Risk & Limits' percentage blocks become the two books. - Lab and Pluto now differ on purpose: Pluto's sales are limits at the mid that are cancelled if they do not fill; the lab assumes a fill at the mid, so it keeps a size limit for realism.
- The portfolio simulator feels the lab's bid-size cap only where a ticker has an observed day of its own chain (its drawn quotes carry the observed bid size); a ticker without one, and every LEAPS, get synthetic quotes of size 1,000,000, so the cap never binds there. ## Order execution platform (started 2026-08-24)

**Decision**: IBKR is the source of truth for positions, not the app's own database — the app places real orders (paper Gateway) and continuously syncs IBKR's reported state back into Postgres. **Why**: a real prod bug (AAOI's naked covered call from a free-typed quantity field with no cross-leg validation) showed manual position entry could silently drift from reality; the fix was to remove manual entry entirely in favor of real order placement.

**Adaptive is single-leg only (Marcelo, 2026-09-29; on prod).** Plain-language: IBKR's "fill priority" (Urgent/Normal/Patient) only works on one-part orders, so two-part orders (Covered Call entries, rolls, covered-call closes) no longer offer or send it; they are plain limit orders on the net price, filled together or not at all. IBKR documents Adaptive as unavailable for spreads, and all 19 filled combos in the book filled at exactly the net limit (single-leg Adaptive beat the limit on 8 of 28 option sells, ~$4/order on paper). Worker `buildOrder` sends the BAG without `algoStrategy`; confirm stores a priority only on single-leg orders; Order Review hides the picker on multi-leg orders. Signals order form (Marcelo approved): when the contracts need more shares than are free (a Covered Call entry), the picker is hidden and the expected net Edge / Edge $ / premium assume a fill at the mid (Patient's assumption), since the combo rests at its mid-built net limit; the signal snapshot records `adaptivePriority: null` for it.

**What's built and verified against real paper trades** (opens, fills, a full close, a cancel of a live order, rolls, and reconciliation-driven self-healing have all been confirmed against real IBKR activity):
- `order_requests` table — the web-to-worker order queue (`pending_confirmation → confirmed → submitted → filled|partially_filled|cancelled|rejected|error`).
- `src/ibkr/ibkrGatewayPersistentConnection.ts` — the app's one long-lived IBKR connection (fixed `clientId=42`, everywhere else uses per-request random ids), reconnects with backoff.
- `src/ibkrGatewayWorker.ts` (separate `worker:` process, deployed to the VPS not Heroku — see [[project_worker_runs_on_vps]]) — places orders (single-leg, or an atomic BAG combo for multi-leg), tracks fills, and runs `reconcilePositionsFromIbkr()` every 60s + on connect, with an in-flight mutex so passes never overlap. Pairing heuristic: long stock + short call same symbol → `covered_call`; any short put → `cash_secured_put`, handled unconditionally and independently of a covered call on the same symbol (fixed 2026-08-27 — a real AAOI position had its put stranded as `unstructured` forever after its sibling call/stock rolled off into a separate covered_call position, since the put was only ever considered when no stock/calls existed at all); anything else → `unstructured` ("Needs Review" badge, never hidden). Each distinct short-call conId now splits into its own position + proportional stock slice (fixed 2026-08-25 after MU showed 2 calls wrongly merged into one position).
- **2026-08-27: `ibkrGateway*` naming convention adopted.** Every file whose code ships as part of the VPS-deployed worker (`ibkrGatewayWorker.ts` and its `src/ibkr/ibkrGateway*.ts` imports) is prefixed `ibkrGateway`, so it's obvious at a glance which edits need a manual `npm run deploy:worker`, not just a `git push`.
- **2026-08-27: worker silently disconnected for 8+ hours, root-caused and fixed.** `ibkrGatewayWorker.ts`'s persistent IBKR connection dropped at 12:50 UTC; its reconnect loop logged one failed attempt then went silent forever, even though `systemctl status` still showed the process "active (running)". Root cause: `ibkrGatewayTunnel.ts`'s `openIbkrTunnel()` awaited `sshClient.connect()` with no timeout — a stalled SSH attempt left that promise permanently pending, freezing the reconnect loop with no further logs (distinct from the already-fixed unhandled-pipe-error crash noted below). While disconnected, IBKR-side closes on 4 MU/AAOI legs never synced back to `position_legs`, surfacing as false "position reconciliation" alerts from the separate hourly health-check connection (which uses its own short-lived connection via `connectIbkr.ts` and kept working throughout). **Fixed**: `openIbkrTunnel()` now has its own 15s timeout on the SSH connect phase. - `src/routes/positions.ts` — build-then-confirm pattern everywhere: `POST /orders` (preview, writes nothing) → `POST /orders/:id/confirm` (transmit gate, `pg_notify`s the worker) → `POST /orders/:id/cancel` (works pre-submission instantly; once submitted, flips to `cancel_requested` and the worker calls `ib.cancelOrder()` — confirmed against a real live order). Roll and close follow the same pattern. Close accepts `contractsToClose`, option-leg-driven (stock quantity always derived, never independently settable).

The 2 already-corrupted AMAT/SPCX legs closed via a one-off script (`scripts/closeOrphanedRolledStockLegs.ts`, zero P&L — shares were never sold, just re-tagged by the roll), confirmed via direct prod query (`close_reason='closed_via_app'`, exit_price=entry_price, quantities now match IBKR exactly). ## Strategy domain notes (from Juan Dominguez, non-developer trading-domain user, via WhatsApp 2026-08-12)
Captured for the trade-alert-generation algorithm design — don't lose it.
- **Trade timing**: avoid executing right at market open; wait ~30 min. Anecdotal: Nasdaq direction after the first 30-min candle often predicts the day's trend (unverified). A sharp opening drop may mean CSP premiums pay more that day, and vice versa for calls.
- **Covered calls**: sell against anything held, always, ideally bought on a dip first. Generally *want* assignment (premium + appreciation to strike = max profit) — varies per ticker based on comfort holding long-term if not assigned. Higher/further strike = less premium now but keeps the stock for continued call-selling.
- **Cash-secured puts**: assignment usually *not* wanted. Worth checking moving averages/trend as a screening filter. Worst-case defense if assigned and stock drops: sell covered calls against the assigned shares to work back to breakeven.
- **Rolling**: when a short option has decayed to near-worthless, often worth buying it back even at a small cost to free up collateral, then selling further out for fresh premium.
- **Scenario comparison**: when choosing between candidate strikes, want P&L outcomes compared side by side under a price scenario — satisfied today by Trade Alerts' payoff-summary cards; a full interactive chart per candidate is deferred.
- **Overall framing**: covered calls are comparatively lower-risk; CSPs need more scrutiny since assignment means acquiring stock at strike. System objective: maximize premium income plus the "wheel" cycle (assigned → redeploy cash → repeat). No schema implication — operational philosophy executed via Screener/Trade Alerts.

Heroku-to-VPS bridge is an SSH tunnel with a dedicated, restricted key (`permitopen`+`command=` clause, `restrict` alone does not block exec-channel command execution). Two separate Gateway containers (`ib-gateway-paper`/`ib-gateway-live`) for independent restart schedules and crash isolation.
  - VPS live since 2026-08-13 at `142.132.185.128`. IBKR does not support a second/secondary user on a paper account — multi-user needs the live account. Juan owns his own IBKR credentials end-to-end.

Made strategy-agnostic 2026-08-25 — `strategy_key` dropped from `shortlist_entries` (a shortlisted ticker is now scanned by every strategy's trade-alert job), after discovering CSP alerts never fired because no ticker had ever been shortlisted under that strategy.
- Shared `Spinner` component standardized for every non-immediate operation. See [[feedback_ui_loading_and_modal_dismiss_conventions]].

- Ticker Detail modal scoped around live pricing/chart/option chain (not fundamentals), platform-wide shared modal for every ticker symbol in the app.

- Authentication: password-based, Argon2id, `express-session`+`connect-pg-simple` (Postgres, not Redis). No self-service password reset — CLI script only. Production sign-in moves to passkey-only (see the Passkey section; switched by `PASSKEY_LOGIN`).
- **IV Rank formula** (approved 2026-08-21): `(current IV − min IV) / (max IV − min IV) × 100` over up to a trailing 252-trading-day window. Shown with the actual window size (e.g. "IV Rank (45d)") rather than hidden until a full year accumulates. Requires ≥2 distinct-IV points and a non-zero range, else null.
- **Yield/ranking formula** (approved): `(premium/capitalAtRisk)×(365/dte)`, annualized. See "Currently open" above for the follow-up discussion on what it doesn't account for.
- **Roll trigger thresholds** (approved 2026-08-21): close/roll when the leg has decayed to ≤50% of credit collected (tastytrade's "50% rule") OR DTE drops to ≤21, whichever first. No delta-based "avoid assignment" trigger — no rigorous backing found and it cuts against covered calls generally *wanting* assignment.
- **Roll IV-rank gate + net-credit floor** (approved 2026-08-31, built same day — `src/ibkr/generateRollCandidates.ts`, `src/lib/rollEconomics.ts`). Two refinements to the roll trigger above: (1) the 21-DTE trigger is a gamma/pin-risk control and fires unconditionally; the 50%-decay trigger is a discretionary profit-take and is now gated on the ticker's IV Rank ≥ 30 (top ~70% of its own 252-day range) — rolling early into a low-IV, cheap-premium environment isn't worth it. A ticker without enough IV history (`ivRank` null) is not gated, same "absence of data isn't evidence against" convention as `calendarUnverified`. (2) A roll is only ever suggested when `replacement.premium − closeLeg.currentPrice` (net credit) exceeds a **measured, not guessed**, minimum: 2× the account's own trailing average per-contract option commission (from `trades.commission`, IBKR's real `CommissionReport` — never a hardcoded published rate) + half the bid/ask spread on both the closing and replacement legs (read directly off the live quote, not assumed). Below 5 historical option fills, the commission component is $0 (no data, no penalty) rather than substituted with a guessed rate. If no candidate in the strategy's delta/DTE band clears this floor, no roll is suggested at all — Juan's explicit call: that's an acceptable outcome, not an error; he can hold to expiry/assignment or act manually. Deliberately does **not** attempt to guarantee a roll beats *not* rolling over the position's remaining life — that depends on future price/IV paths no formula can know; see the backlog below for that as a separate, explicitly probabilistic, low-priority idea. `RollSuggestion`/`RollStructure`/rationale text/Telegram formatting all surface `netCredit`/`requiredMinimumCredit`; refresh re-validates both and flags drift (`stillTriggered`/`stillNetCredit`) the same way. Both repos typecheck clean; not yet live-tested against a real triggered roll (no pending roll alert this session).
- **Allocation/exposure formula** (approved 2026-08-25): every % is against total account net liquidation value (positions + cash), not sum-of-positions, with an explicit "Unallocated" cash row.

## Atomic release phase: backup, restore on failure (2026-10-12, `35f80d7`; prod from the 2026-10-12 promotion)

**In plain words:** a promotion is now one click and all-or-nothing. Before changing the database, the release checks it is safe to deploy and takes a database backup. If anything fails after the database changes, it restores that backup and keeps the old code, so the app, the database and the trading worker never end up mismatched.

- **Order** (`scripts/run-release-phase.ts`, Procfile `release: npm run release-phase`):
  1. Deploy guard. On prod it stops before anything changes when the market is open, during the 15 minutes after the close, or with an order in flight; staging only warns.
  2. Heroku Postgres backup, only when there are migrations to run (`HEROKU_RELEASE_API_KEY`, Juan's key, used for backup and restore only).
  3. Migrations, in one transaction.
  4. Worker deploy over SSH.
  5. On a failure after step 3: drop the tables this release created, restore the backup, check the table list and the latest migration, exit 1.
  6. Record the backup in `release_backups`.
  7. Fill `major_macro_events` once when it is empty.
- **Restore is refused** (the release still fails, with a Telegram alert, fix forward) when orders or trades were written after the backup, or when the worker deploy broke off mid-way, so its state is unknown.
- **Previously** the migrations committed before the guard and the worker deploy ran, so an aborted release left the old code on the new schema.
- **Verified on staging 2026-10-12:**
  - With `RELEASE_PHASE_TEST_FAILURE=after_migrations` (staging-only switch), v192 took backup b025, migrated, failed on purpose and restored. Afterwards the migrations, tables and order/trade/position counts matched the baseline, and staging stayed on v191.
  - Then a clean release, v193: backup b027 → migration → staging worker redeployed over SSH (e11675c → 35f80d7, healthy, bound DUR854038) → `release_backups` row written.
- **Limits:**
  - Heroku's Roll back runs the *target* release's release command, so automatic restore on Roll back only works for releases made from a build that contains this script. The first prod promotion's rollback target (`7c0e137`) does not.
  - The worker still builds `origin/main`, not the promoted commit; a mismatch sends a Telegram warning. Do not push to main during a promotion.
- **Heroku quirk:** a config change after a FAILED release re-runs that failed build's release (seen on staging v193), so after a failed prod promotion any `config:set` retries the promotion.
- **Dropped (Marcelo 2026-11-04): automatic restore on Roll back.** It would erase every order, fill and position written since the release; a failed release already restores its own backup, and a schema change is reverted by hand when needed.

## Pluto — the trading bot, as it runs today (rewritten 2026-11-04; live on staging's paper account since 2026-10-06, Vol Farmer v4 since 2026-11-01; not on prod)
Plain language: Pluto trades on its own, following Vol Farmer v4's rules written in code (the same rules the Backtesting lab runs), on the tickers switched on for it and within its own share of the account. No AI model is involved any more: from 2026-10-06 to 2026-10-31 a language model picked trades from the Signals, and that history, with the design that led to it, is in PROGRESS-ARCHIVE.md section D. What v4 trades is in "Vol Farmer v4 (lab and Pluto)" above, and the quote plumbing it reads is in "Pluto's Vol Farmer plumbing". This section covers the bot around them.
- **Where it runs.** Its own `agent` process (Procfile, `src/plutoAgent.ts`), which exits at once unless `PLUTO_ENABLED=true`. It talks to the API over HTTP as a service user (admin), so its orders go through the same routes and gates as a person's. One agent at a time, by a lease renewed every 15 s (`pluto_agent_lease`); the confirm route refuses an order from an agent without it. Heartbeat in `worker_health` (`pluto_agent`) every 45 s. Prod has the code but no agent running and no Pluto account (see Currently open).
- **On, paused, breakers.** `pluto_state`: off/on, paused or not, and the tripped breakers; it trades only when on, not paused and no breaker. A release keeps the state on staging and pauses Pluto on prod; several restarts of one release within an hour pause it (crash loop). Breakers (daily loss, reconciliation discrepancy, order error, fill slippage) each wait for a person to reset them and then Resume. The platform's trading halt stops it too.
- **The day.** A trading window (settings `windowStartEt`/`windowEndEt`, ending at least 30 minutes before IBKR's close for the day, half days included); working orders are cancelled 2 minutes before the close. The opening look runs every ticker once the day's first option band quotes are in (at 10:30 ET without them, flagged incomplete and announced). On a day no ticker has an option expiring at the close (Options expiry "today", usually Tuesdays and Thursdays) the band has nothing to quote, so the look runs at once with an ℹ️ notice and no warning (2026-11-04). After that, a ticker is looked at again whenever a newer band-quote cycle has finished for it, and every ticker after a settings change, an order's end, a cooldown's end or a closed position. Rounds never overlap; a failed round is retried once.
- **A round.** System checks first (Pluto's state, trading halt, market open, today's close read from IBKR, inside the window, trading worker up, account data, daily loss, reconciliation); any failure skips the round. A SPY fall past the stress limit holds new opens (sales and LEAPS still go) unless an admin overrides it for the day. Then each ticker, in the day's rotating alphabetical order, unless frozen for a pending split or cooling down after a fill: v4's steps decide, a fresh quote prices each order at the mid, the gates check it (live quote, v4's bid and spread rules, delta, Pluto's capital budget, Safety Limits), and the order is sent. A second pass sends the LEAPS due, on the cash left. In the window's last 2 minutes only the in-the-money Call buybacks go, when that rule is on.
- **Money.** Each ticker's pot is its allocation × (1 + excess allowance) × Pluto's capital budget % × account value; a ticker with no allocation shares what the others leave. All tickers draw on one cash pool: the least of Pluto's free budget, the account's free cash and its settled cash (a cash account; on paper total cash stands in for settled cash).
- **Orders.** Single-leg orders are limit orders at the mid with IBKR's Adaptive algo (Normal); a two-part order (shares and a Call) is a plain limit on the net. Pluto watches each one, cancels it 2 minutes before the close (the platform's unfilled-order sweep may cancel it sooner), and plans an unfilled one again at a later look. Routine refusals (price moved, no quote, tick size, margin, settled cash) are retried later with no breaker; any other error trips the order-error breaker; a fill further than `maxFillSlippagePct` from the expected price trips fill slippage.
- **Settings** (Pluto screen; changing them, switching on, resuming, resetting a breaker and the stress override are admin only): capital budget and excess allowance, window, daily-loss limit, quote age, fill slippage, quote timing, the seven Safety Limits, Telegram verbosity, and v4's 24 rule values. Per ticker: allocation % and Always hold the stock. A ticker is switched on by its Pluto switch on the Shortlist (capped by `maxEnabledTickers`). Every change is audited; each round records its settings version.
- **Readiness.** On market days at 6:00 ET (re-checked every 10 minutes while failing, final at 9:20 ET): IBKR connection, the API sign-in, and whether Pluto is running. A failure still there at 9:20 pauses a running Pluto. The platform's own readiness also checks Pluto's cash.
- **Splits.** `pluto_split_check` (09:00 UTC on market days) records splits and freezes a ticker until its rewrite; at each look a price jump the size of a split with no split recorded stops that ticker for the day, with a Telegram notice.
- **Telegram.** Pluto's own bot in the alerts group: starts and stops, deploy and crash-loop pauses, readiness, a late opening look, three failed rounds in a row and the recovery, breaker trips and every switch, pause or resume made on the screen. Each order sent and its outcome, and the split notices, only when verbosity is "actions".
- **Screen.** Pluto page tabs read `/pluto/*`: state, scoreboard, settings and their audit, ticker settings, Vol Farmer v4 book, How it works, allocations, passes, actions, orders, events, tickers.
